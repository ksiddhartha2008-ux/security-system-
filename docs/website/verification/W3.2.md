## PLAN

Confirm that detections sharing a website, source address or actor produce one incident, that severity and priority escalate, that the title follows the highest severity rule, and that notifications are throttled with a recorded reason.


## PAUSE

```text
✔ a real batch produces one detection, one incident and is idempotent on replay
✔ the attack corpus is detected with measured precision and recall
```

Observed:

- First detection: `w-auth-001`, high, risk above 70, incident P3.
- Success after failures: `w-auth-003`, critical. The same incident becomes critical, P2. Incident count stays at one.
- Later detections attach through `incident_detections`, whose primary key is the detection id, so a retry cannot attach twice.
- In the corpus test, at least one notification is suppressed and every suppressed row has a reason.

## INTEGRATE

The priority formula was checked by hand: severity 4, criticality 3, raw 11, P2. This matches the acceptance scenario. The entity rule was checked by ensuring the access denial and injection detections joined the incident created for the same address.

## ACT

The browser walkthrough showed the incident title still naming the first rule after escalation. The title now follows the highest severity rule (commit `60e02c8`). This is a real defect found by the walkthrough, not by the tests; a test assertion for the title would be a useful addition.

## RECORD

W6 is VERIFIED.

## Notification behaviour

- One notification per incident per 15 minutes unless priority increases.
- Hourly organization cap, default 60.
- Suppression is stored with `status = 'suppressed'` and a reason. The incident is never suppressed.
- No email or webhook is sent. The channel is `in_app`.
